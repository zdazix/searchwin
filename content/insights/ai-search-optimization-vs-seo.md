## The short version

SEO gets a page ranked in a list of links. AI search optimization gets a business named in an answer. The two overlap more than the names suggest, and both are only useful if the visit turns into an enquiry.

The shift is already large. Google says more than 2 billion people a month use AI Overviews, the AI answer above its search results ([Alphabet, Q2 2025 earnings call](https://abc.xyz/2025-q2-earnings-call)). When an AI summary appears, people click a regular result on 8% of visits, against 15% when there is none ([Pew Research Center, July 2025](https://www.pewresearch.org/short-reads/2025/07/22/google-users-are-less-likely-to-click-on-links-when-an-ai-summary-appears-in-the-results/)).

## What stays the same

AI tools read the web through search and through their own crawlers. So the base of SEO still holds:

- A site that crawlers can reach and read.
- Pages that say clearly what you do, for whom and where.
- Other sites that link to you and talk about you.

If these are broken, nothing built on top of them works, in Google or in AI.

## The four things that change

### 1. The answer is a shortlist

A search result offers ten links and lets the reader compare. An AI answer names a few businesses and gives a reason for each. There is no page two. You are either one of the names or you are not part of the decision.

### 2. Sources outside your site count for more

To decide who to name, AI leans on what other sites say: review platforms, directories, comparison articles, mentions in publications. A business with a perfect website and no outside confirmation is hard for a model to recommend.

That makes listings, reviews and mentions part of the work, not an extra.

### 3. Questions get longer and more specific

People type “dentist austin” into a search box. They ask AI “who is the best dentist in Austin for implants, I want someone I can trust”. The longer question carries more intent, and it needs a page that answers exactly that.

We cover how to find those questions in [High-intent questions: what buyers ask AI right before they choose](/blog/insights/high-intent-questions/).

### 4. Success is measured differently

Rankings and traffic do not tell you whether AI names you. The measure is direct: ask the same questions on the same engines at regular intervals, and record who is named and in what order. Then count the enquiries that follow, by page.

## Side by side

| | SEO | AI search optimization |
|---|---|---|
| Result | A page in a list of links | A business named in an answer |
| Unit of work | Keyword | Buyer question |
| What decides it | The page and its links | The page, plus what other sources say |
| Where the work is | Mostly your site | Your site and outside sources |
| How you check | Rankings, traffic | The same questions re-asked; enquiries by page |

## Where to start

1. **Let the crawlers in.** Check that robots.txt does not block the crawlers AI search tools use, such as OAI-SearchBot and PerplexityBot, and that your key pages are indexed.
2. **Make your facts agree.** Name, address, phone, hours, services and prices should be the same on your site, your business profile and every listing.
3. **Build pages for choosing questions.** One question per page, with the answer first and one next step. See [Anatomy of an organic conversion page](/blog/insights/organic-conversion-page-anatomy/).
4. **Earn outside confirmation.** Reviews, listings in the directories your buyers use, and mentions in publications your category reads.
5. **Measure answers and enquiries.** Re-ask your questions every month and track which pages your leads come from.

None of this replaces SEO. It finishes the job SEO started: being found is half of it, being chosen is the rest.
